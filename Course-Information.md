## 2. Schedule and Class Format

- **Credits (Hours):** 3 (3)
- **Intended For:** First-year students
- **Class Times:** Monday period 4, Thursday periods 1-2
- **Classroom:** Nongshim International Hall Room 208
- **Class Format:** The activity types in the syllabus are lecture, presentation, and lab, with the following details.
  1. Lectures are given using the textbook.
  2. Time is set aside for answering questions about the lecture content or for presentations.
  3. Classes are held in person in principle, and in unavoidable cases in-person classes, online classes, and recorded lectures are combined.
- **Course Content:** The Week 1 course introduction material describes the course content as follows.
  1. Understanding Python: the basics of programming and selection statements, loops, functions, objects, and so on.
  2. Theory and practice: each week, lab problems related to the theory class are prepared and worked through.
  3. Connection with other languages: when needed, classes are connected with the C language.
- **Attendance:** The syllabus marks the course as subject to optional attendance checking, and the Week 1 course introduction material announced that attendance counts toward the grade ([3. Assessment](#3-assessment)).
- **Exam Supervision:** Exams are not held unsupervised.
- **Class Description:** The course aims to teach Python, a representative programming language used in a wide range of fields.
- **Course and Departmental Objectives:** A general studies course for first-year students, designed to deepen the understanding of programs and to build implementation skills.
- **Study Objectives:**
  1. Understand Python.
  2. Understand programming and analyze other programs.
  3. Implement new ideas.
- **Design Objectives:** The class design consists of lectures and presentations.
  1. Lecture: Centered on the designated textbook, lectures proceed interactively, not only through the instructor's lecture but also through questions from students.
  2. Presentation: Centered on what was learned in the previous class, students write new programs themselves and discuss them with each other.
- **Core Competency:** The syllabus marks the relevance of the course to the Practicality competency as very high.
- **Prerequisites:** The syllabus designates no recommended prerequisites or enrollment requirements.
- **Lab Environment:** Code is written and run in IDLE, which comes with the Python installed from [python.org](https://www.python.org/). Installation and usage are summarized in [Lecture 2](Lecture-Notes/Lec02_Getting-Started-with-Python/Concepts_Lecture.md).

## 3. Assessment

| Item | Weight |
|:-----|-----:|
| Occasional quizzes | 10% |
| Midterm presentation | 40% |
| Final exam | 40% |
| Participation | 10% |

- The table above is the assessment scheme in the syllabus. The syllabus marks the scores as disclosed.
- The assessment plan in the syllabus is to hold a midterm exam and a final exam and, if needed, a project assignment related to the class content. Exams are held in person in principle and may be replaced by online exams or assignments in consideration of social distancing.
- The Week 1 course introduction material (2024-03-04) announced, with the proviso that it might change, an assessment of 90% exams and 10% attendance. The exams are coding exams, with the midterm at 40% and the final at 50%. Missing one third or more of the classes results in an F.

## 4. Course Policies

- **Academic Ethics:** Students who take part in the university's educational activities have the following general duties in every part of completing the curriculum, including attending lectures, preparing and submitting assignments, and taking exams.
  1. Participate actively in all learning with a sincere and honest mind and a passionate attitude, in line with the purpose of the university and its educational philosophy.
  2. Be aware of one's position as an educated person receiving higher education, and devote oneself to creative academic inquiry based on moral sensitivity.
  3. Faithfully keep the promises made to faculty or fellow students in connection with educational activities.
  4. Respect the personality of faculty and fellow students and observe basic courtesy.
- If credits are found to have been obtained through dishonest acts, such as plagiarizing assignments, reusing assignments from other classes, or cheating on exams, or through other improper means, the credits are canceled even if they have already been granted, and disciplinary actions such as reprimand, suspension, expulsion, or dismissal may be taken in consideration of the circumstances.
- The university operates a plagiarism detection system (SafeAssign on Blackboard) to raise awareness of academic ethics and to encourage creativity, and students must check in advance whether an assignment constitutes plagiarism when submitting it.
